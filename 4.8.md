The paper's conclusion describes B4 as achieving near-100% utilization and discusses the production experience and performance improvements.

# 5. Takeaway #4 — What did B4 teach them?

This is perhaps the **most important conclusion**.

At first, you might think the difficult part is:

> "Can we calculate the optimal traffic distribution?"

B4 showed that the optimization algorithm itself wasn't necessarily the biggest problem.

The real production challenges included:

```text
        Centralized TE
             │
             ↓
      Controller decision
             │
             ↓
       OpenFlow / OFC
             │
             ↓
          OFA
             │
             ↓
       Switch hardware
```

The **control/data-plane bridge and hardware programming** can become bottlenecks.

And the outage demonstrated that control-path scalability and failure recovery are critical. 

So:

> **A mathematically good network solution is not enough. The whole control system must work reliably in the real world.**

# 7. Your final 45–60 second conclusion

For the actual seminar, I recommend saying something like this:

> **"To conclude, B4 demonstrated that centralized traffic engineering can significantly improve the efficiency of a production WAN when the network environment is well controlled. Google combined centralized TE with SDN and OpenFlow while retaining traditional routing mechanisms for integration and fallback. The system achieved very high link utilization and measurable throughput improvements. However, the production outage showed that centralized control also introduces new engineering challenges. The controller-to-switch communication path, hardware programming, failure recovery, and operational complexity are all critical. That's a strong ending because it gives both:
